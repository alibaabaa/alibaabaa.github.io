Starting

Every engagement begins with a call.

You describe what is being asked of you, and who is answering it now. What comes back is a straight view on whether this is work worth doing, and whether it is work for someone else.

Nothing is diagnosed on that call. An hour is not long enough to be right, and being wrong early is expensive for both sides.

Where people usually start

Three ways in, smallest first.

Something with a date attached. A customer questionnaire, a tender response, an ISO 27001 cycle, a Cyber Resilience Act gap that has to close. Defined scope and a known end. This is the most common way work begins.

A read on where practice actually stands. Interviews across engineering, delivery and leadership, alongside the documents. The output is a written picture of current practice and where it differs from what is written down. It stands on its own and commits you to nothing after it.

Designing a practice and putting it into use. The larger piece. An operating model sized to the team, put into use by the people who will run it, with evidence produced by the delivery work rather than assembled afterwards.

How it is scoped and paid for

Each stage is a fixed fee, agreed before it starts.

The fee for the next stage is set when the current one closes, from what that stage found rather than from an estimate made before anyone looked.

You can stop at the end of any stage with something finished in your hands.

After the work

Some obligations recur whether or not anyone is engaged.

ISO 27001 requires an internal audit every year. Surveillance audits come round. Products change, and so does what is being asked of them.

Where that is useful, it is a scheduled piece of work at a fixed fee, booked in advance with a defined scope. Not a standing monthly arrangement.

Working with one person

Bedrock Cyber is one person, which has consequences worth knowing before you start.

  • The work is done by whoever you spoke to on the call. Nothing is handed to someone junior once the fee is agreed.
  • Availability is finite. Start dates are agreed rather than assumed, and a date given is a date held.
  • Where the work needs something outside product security, penetration testing being the usual case, you will be told and pointed at someone who does it well.
